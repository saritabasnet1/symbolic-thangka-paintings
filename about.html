About This Journal

The Symbolic Thangka Paintings Journal is an independent educational journal about symbolic thangka art. It is not a shop, and it is not affiliated with any gallery, dealer or maker. Its purpose is to help readers understand what symbolic thangkas show and why they are painted the way they are.

The journal focuses on teaching thangkas — narrative paintings that illustrate Buddhist philosophy and history. Its main subjects are the Wheel of Life, the Life of Buddha and the Jataka tales. These paintings are made to teach rather than to portray a single deity, and the journal treats them as visual texts that can be read, scene by scene, with patience and attention.

What the Journal Does

Each article explains a subject in plain language, describes the parts of a painting in order, and connects those parts to the teaching they illustrate. The journal describes the craft as well as the meaning: handmade painting on cotton canvas, in mineral colour with gold detail, with many small figures arranged across registers and landscapes, each gesture and attribute following scriptural description.

The journal does not invent stories about individual paintings, makers or owners. It stays with what can be described from the painting itself and from the teaching it illustrates. When a particular painting is mentioned as an example — for instance, a Wheel of Life painting in a semi-antique style, on canvas, measuring 50 by 70 centimetres — it is mentioned only as an example of the type, with the size and material given so the reader can picture the scale.

Who Is This Journal For?

The journal is written for readers who are curious about Buddhist art and want to understand it better: students, teachers, collectors who want to look more carefully, and anyone who has seen a thangka in a monastery, a study room or a home shrine and wondered what its many small scenes mean.

No prior knowledge is assumed. Terms are explained as they appear, and articles link to one another so a reader can follow a subject from its first introduction to a fuller reading.

How the Journal Is Organised

The Blog holds five long articles. The first introduces symbolic thangka painting as a whole. The next three take one subject each — the Wheel of Life, the Life of Buddha and the Jataka tales — and read it step by step. The last gives practical guidance on choosing, caring for and displaying a symbolic thangka at home. The Services page describes the educational guides the journal offers, and the Contact page is the place to send questions and suggestions.

Start on the Home page if you are new here, or go straight to the first article, Symbolic Thangka Paintings: Meaning, History and Buddhist Teaching.
