Educational Guides and Services

This journal offers educational guides, not sales. Each guide is a way of learning to look at a symbolic thangka more carefully. The guides below describe what the journal covers, and each one links to the long articles where the subject is explained in full.

Reading Symbolism

A guide to the visual language of symbolic thangkas: how gestures, objects, colours and small figures carry meaning, and how to work through a painting part by part instead of trying to take it in all at once. The guide explains the difference between a symbol that points to a teaching and a decorative detail, and it shows how the same symbol can appear in different paintings with a related but not identical meaning.

Begin with Symbolic Thangka Paintings: Meaning, History and Buddhist Teaching, which introduces the language of teaching paintings, and continue with Wheel of Life in Symbolic Thangka: Reading the Bhavachakra Step by Step, which puts that language to work on a single painting.

Understanding Teaching Thangkas

A guide to the main teaching subjects: the Wheel of Life, the Life of Buddha and the Jataka tales. For each subject, the guide explains what story or teaching the painting illustrates, how the scenes are arranged, and what a teacher might point to when using the painting in a monastery, a study room or a home shrine.

Read Life of Buddha Thangka: How a Painting Tells the Buddha's Story for the narrative order of the Buddha's life, and Jataka Tales in Thangka Art: Stories, Symbols and Moral Lessons for the stories of earlier lives and the moral lessons drawn from them.

Choosing and Caring for a Painting

A guide to looking before you choose, and to caring for a painting once it is in your home. It covers how to look at composition, condition and craftsmanship, how to think about size and subject, and how to hang, protect and clean a painting on cotton canvas with mineral colour and gold detail.

The full guidance is in How to Choose, Care for and Display a Symbolic Thangka at Home. That article includes one verified example of scale and type — a Wheel of Life painting, semi-antique in style, on canvas, 50 by 70 centimetres — to help readers picture what a teaching painting of that size looks like in a room.

What Are These Guides Not?

These guides do not sell paintings, value paintings, or recommend a particular painting to buy. They do not offer spiritual advice or make claims about what a painting will do for its owner. Their aim is narrower and, for this journal, more useful: to help a reader see what is actually in the painting, understand what it teaches, and care for it well.

If you have a question about a guide, or a suggestion for a subject the journal should explain next, please use the Contact page. To read the guides in full, start with the Blog.
